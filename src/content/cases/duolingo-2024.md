## The role

A Duolingo spokesperson said the company uses GPT to translate sentences.[^techcrunch-2024] Duolingo had 600 full-time employees in 2022, according to company filings.[^techcrunch-2024-2]

## The change

Duolingo confirmed that it cut around 10% of its contractor workforce at the end of 2023.[^techcrunch-2024-3] The company disputed calling the departures layoffs.[^techcrunch-2024-4] It said only a small minority of contractors were let go as their projects wrapped.[^techcrunch-2024-4] The company also confirmed to Bloomberg that part of the reason it no longer needed as many people was attributable to AI.[^techcrunch-2024-5]

## What happened next

On 28 April 2025, Duolingo's cofounder and chief executive, Luis von Ahn, posted an email to all employees on LinkedIn.[^fortune-2025] According to von Ahn, the company will gradually stop using contractors to do work that AI can handle.[^techco-2025] A week later, he said he did not see AI replacing what employees do.[^fortune-2025-2]

Von Ahn told the Financial Times that he did not expect the amount of blowback.[^fortune-2025-3]

## Open questions

- Will Duolingo disclose how many contractors remain, or whether further contractor work moves to AI?
- Will users or staff report errors in AI-generated lessons that human review did not catch?
- Will Duolingo bring back human content staff or contractors?
