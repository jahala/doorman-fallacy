## The role

TikTok is expected to cut around 300 jobs in Ireland as part of a wider restructuring of its global trust and safety division.[^aa] Overall, the restructuring is expected to result in a net reduction of around 300 jobs.[^aa-2]

The majority of the affected roles are within the trust and safety division.[^irishtimes-aug] Also at risk are roles in the artificial intelligence data service and operations team, responsible for annotation and search operations services.[^irishtimes-aug-2] Content moderators are responsible for keeping dangerous and harmful content off the platform.[^yahoo-independent] Dublin is a hub for TikTok's trust and safety operations, including content moderation and data protection.[^irishexaminer]

Under the plan, 667 jobs at the Grand Canal Dock office were placed at risk.[^irishtimes-aug-3] Under the plan, the company intends to create 321 new roles, with opportunities for affected employees to apply.[^irishtimes-aug-4]

## The change

On 1 July 2026, the company said a collective consultation process with staff was under way.[^aa-3] It said it could not provide precise figures on job losses at that stage.[^aa-3] TikTok said the cuts came as part of a reorganisation to strengthen its global operating model for trust and safety, including through the latest technological innovations.[^yahoo-independent-2]

Under the plan, work undertaken by the affected employees in Dublin will be redistributed to TikTok operations in other countries, outsourced to vendor companies and handled by artificial intelligence-enabled or automated solutions, among other measures.[^irishtimes-aug-5] Some of the workload will be incorporated into the responsibilities of newly created Dublin-based roles.[^irishtimes-aug-6] Certain types of current Dublin-based roles will be eliminated completely under the proposed restructuring.[^irishtimes-aug-7] The restructuring would shift internal resources away from legacy frontline tasks towards higher-skilled, judgement-based and specialised positions.[^aa-4]

In the first quarter, automated systems detected and removed 178 million, or 96.7 per cent, of the 184 million videos taken down globally, before human moderators reviewed them.[^koreatimes] Overall, 99.3 per cent of removed videos were taken down before users reported them.[^koreatimes-2] TikTok said its systems automatically remove content only when they are highly confident that it violates the platform's rules.[^koreatimes-3]

## What happened next

On 20 July 2026, the trade union representing employees at the Irish arm of TikTok said the planned 300 job cuts would have a significant knock-on impact on the safety of users on the platform.[^irishexaminer-2] The Communications Workers' Union called on the company to significantly reduce the number of job losses at its Dublin hub and wrote an open letter calling for properly resourced trust and safety departments and greater regulatory oversight of the platform.[^irishexaminer-3]

On 19 August 2026, about 300 jobs looked set to be cut at TikTok's Dublin office.[^irishtimes-aug-8] Following a consultation process with employees, the company offered a finalised mutual termination agreement to some affected workers.[^irishtimes-aug-9] Employees who sign the agreement are expected to finish work at TikTok at the end of the month.[^irishtimes-aug-10] One TikTok employee said the process for retention and redeployment, including to newly created roles under the restructuring plan, was largely considered unfair by the affected workers.[^irishtimes-aug-11]

The Korea Times reported that 8.8 million videos removed in the first quarter were restored following further review, about 5 per cent of all videos taken down in the period.[^koreatimes-4]
