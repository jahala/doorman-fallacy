## The role

The majority of the affected roles are within the trust and safety division.[^irishtimes-aug] Also at risk are roles in the artificial intelligence data service and operations team, responsible for annotation and search operations services.[^irishtimes-aug-2] Content moderators are responsible for keeping dangerous and harmful content off the platform.[^yahoo-independent] Dublin is a hub for TikTok's trust and safety operations, including content moderation and data protection.[^irishexaminer]

Under the plan, 667 jobs at the Grand Canal Dock office were placed at risk.[^irishtimes-aug-3] Under the plan, the company intends to create 321 new roles, with opportunities for affected employees to apply.[^irishtimes-aug-4]

## The change

On 1 July 2026, the company said a collective consultation process with staff was under way.[^aa]

Under the plan, work undertaken by the affected employees in Dublin will be redistributed to TikTok operations in other countries, outsourced to vendor companies and handled by artificial intelligence-enabled or automated solutions, among other measures.[^irishtimes-aug-5] Some of the workload will be incorporated into the responsibilities of newly created Dublin-based roles.[^irishtimes-aug-6]

## What happened next

On 20 July 2026, the trade union representing employees at the Irish arm of TikTok said the planned 300 job cuts would have a significant knock-on impact on the safety of users on the platform.[^irishexaminer-2]

On 19 August 2026, about 300 jobs looked set to be cut at TikTok's Dublin office.[^irishtimes-aug-7] Following a consultation process with employees, the company offered a finalised mutual termination agreement to some affected workers.[^irishtimes-aug-8] Employees who sign the agreement are expected to finish work at TikTok at the end of the month.[^irishtimes-aug-9] One TikTok employee said the process for retention and redeployment, including to newly created roles under the restructuring plan, was largely considered unfair by the affected workers.[^irishtimes-aug-10]

The Korea Times reported that 8.8 million videos removed in the first quarter were restored following further review, about 5 per cent of all videos taken down in the period.[^koreatimes]
