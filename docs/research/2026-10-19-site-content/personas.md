# Who the site is for

Composite personas from desk research. None was interviewed; each is a hypothesis until real readers confirm it. Each page states facts about the reader's work, never what they think of the site.

## Reporter, national business newspaper (`journalist`)

**Who and work.** You are a reporter covering technology and work for a national business newspaper. You write several stories a week, often on deadline the same day. When a company announces job cuts linked to AI, you look for the facts, the company's own words, comparable cases elsewhere and people to quote. Your editor requires a source for every claim. Your readers are managers and employees.

**Basis.** Coverage of AI-linked cuts and rehiring in 2026 (Forbes 2026-05-21, Quartz, Washington Times 2026-03-10; links in the private site plan). Daily deadlines and sourcing rules are inference from common newsroom practice.

## Head of customer operations, mid-sized bank (`ops_leader`)

**Who and work.** You run customer operations at a mid-sized bank, with about 300 people in customer service and back-office processing. Leadership has asked for a 15 percent cost reduction next year, and vendors are offering AI agents that answer customer enquiries and check documents. You must present a plan to the executive team with expected savings and risks. Each month you report cost per contact, handling time and customer satisfaction.

**Basis.** Composite. Bank cases in the 2026 sweeps: Commonwealth Bank (voice bot, reversed 2025), DNB (KYC and data control, 2026), HSBC (advisers, reported 2026). Metrics are the standard contact-centre measures; the 15 percent figure is an illustrative inference.

## HR business partner, retail group (`hr_partner`)

**Who and work.** You are an HR business partner at a retail group with about 2,000 employees. When a department is restructured, you run the consultation with employee representatives, check that the legal process is followed, and support the managers who deliver the news. Where you work, redundancies above a certain number require consultation with unions before a final decision.

**Basis.** Composite. Collective consultation duties exist in EU and UK law for collective redundancies; the size of the group is illustrative.

## Customer service adviser, telecom company (`frontline`)

**Who and work.** You work in a customer service team at a telecom company, answering calls and chats. Your manager has told the team that an AI assistant will take routine questions from next quarter and that roles will be reviewed. Your job description says you answer customer enquiries. You have worked there for six years and new colleagues often ask you how to handle unusual cases.

**Basis.** Composite of the customer-service roles in the 2025 to 2026 reversal reports (Klarna, Commonwealth Bank). Not interviewed.

## Union representative, finance sector (`union_rep`)

**Who and work.** You are an elected representative for a finance-sector union. When a bank announces cuts, you meet management, read the reasons it gives, and represent members in the consultation. You prepare written arguments and bring examples from other employers. You also advise members one to one before their individual meetings.

**Basis.** Finance Sector Union's challenge of Commonwealth Bank's voice-bot cut (2025) and Finansforbundet's criticism of DNB's cut (FinansFokus, 2026-10-13), both found in the desk's sweeps.

## Researcher, public policy institute (`researcher`)

**Who and work.** You are a researcher at a public policy institute studying how AI affects employment. You build datasets of employer announcements and what followed, and you publish reports that government departments cite. Your reports state their method, their inclusion rules and their sources, and you reuse data only under a licence that allows it.

**Basis.** Composite. Forecasts on reversals of AI-attributed layoffs by Gartner and Forrester are cited in 2026 coverage; method and licence practice is inference from research norms.

## Lead, AI implementation consultancy (`ai_consultant`)

**Who and work.** You lead a small consultancy that helps companies deploy AI agents in customer service and back-office work. Clients ask how many roles AI can take over and how quickly. You write business cases, run discovery workshops with client teams, and stay involved after go-live. A deployment that fails in public costs your firm the client and its reputation.

**Basis.** Composite; the vendor side of the cases in the ledger. Not interviewed.

## Brand strategist, advertising agency (`strategist`)

**Who and work.** You are a brand strategist at an advertising agency. You read behavioural science and use ideas from Rory Sutherland's Alchemy in client work. You build presentations that explain one idea in a few slides with a memorable example, and you collect examples you can show clients.

**Basis.** Rory Sutherland is vice chairman of Ogilvy UK; Alchemy (2019) is widely used in marketing and strategy work. Inference: this reader is the idea's existing audience.

## Office worker, arrived from a shared link (`passerby`)

**Who and work.** You work in an office job. A colleague shared a link to this site on LinkedIn, and you opened it on your phone on the train home. You have read news about companies replacing staff with AI. You have a few minutes.

**Basis.** Inference: the most common first visit for a site spread by sharing.

## Roles checked

- Does the work with the site: all nine read it; the frontline adviser, union representative and manager also print and use the guides.
- Pays: nobody; the site is free.
- Administers: Jan, as curator; not a reader persona.
- Consumes the result without using the site: employees whose manager uses the before-you-cut guide (covered by the frontline adviser).
- Consulted by law or contract: the union representative and the HR business partner.
- Missing: a regulator or labour inspector, and an investor or analyst following a listed company. Both are smaller audiences; noted, not modelled.
