# "AI rebounding": the GadgetReview article and its sources (2026-11-11)

**Conclusion.** The article ("The AI layoff experiment is backfiring and companies are quietly rehiring humans", Rex Edison, GadgetReview, 2 November 2026) names no company that cut roles for AI and rehired. It retells an SHL survey and two statistics that reach it through CNBC. Its useful leads are two companies CNBC names, Ford and Amazon. The desk rated Ford watch and rejected Amazon.

## The sources behind it

| Claim | Primary source | Checked |
|---|---|---|
| 19% of US workers and 13% of UK workers report first-hand experience of being replaced by AI and later asked back | SHL press release, London, 29 October 2026. Online survey by Propeller Insights of 2,036 employed adults (US 1,029, UK 1,007), June and July 2026 | Read at the source |
| 51% would want a substantial pay rise to return, 42% job-security guarantees, 40% transparency on AI; 8% would refuse | Same release | Read, but the release does not say whether these are shares of all respondents or only of those asked back |
| 32% of organisations that let workers go because of AI have had to rehire staff | Orgvue, "a March report" (2026), linked from CNBC | Not verified: Orgvue's page refuses automated reading (HTTP 403) |
| AI was the primary reason for almost 40% of job cuts announced in May 2026 | Challenger, Gray & Christmas, via CNBC, 5 June 2026 | Read only at CNBC |

CNBC's own figures (30 October 2026, Sophie Caldwell) differ from SHL's release: CNBC gives 6.6% refusing, 43% wanting written guarantees and 30% wanting an apology, none of which appear in the release. Quote SHL's release, not the coverage.

## Named companies

- **Ford.** Ford said it expected AI, with about 900 plant cameras, to carry quality checks that experienced engineers did, and that it underweighted what those engineers knew. It brought in 300 to 350 people over three years. Rated watch: no source gives a number of quality roles cut, and the coverage traces to one Ford press call reported by Bloomberg. The hidden work here (judgment and memory) is reported, not inferred, which makes it a strong case once a primary source is found.
- **Amazon.** Rejected. The cuts removed management layers across many functions, Amazon says AI was not the reason for most of them, and its rehiring is ordinary boomerang hiring.
